## About

I work at the intersection of IT, telecom, cloud infrastructure, and systems architecture. A CKA-certified engineer with over five years of experience in systems integration and technical delivery, I've worked on mission-critical infrastructure involving Nokia and T-Mobile USA, translating complex engineering requirements into working solutions.

My interests extend well beyond the terminal. I'm fascinated by technology, business, geopolitics, history, philosophy, economics, and the strange business of being human.

I'm an engineer by training, a generalist by inclination, and an autodidact by necessity. I like understanding how things work, why they fail, who benefits from their existence, and whether there's a more interesting way to do them.

Currently exploring the territory between **solutions consulting, end-to-end systems architecture, AI, and the art of thinking across disciplines.**

## The Other Hats

My professional identity doesn't fit neatly inside a job description.

I'm interested in product management because technology needs direction, in consulting because difficult problems rarely respect organizational boundaries, in entrepreneurship because sometimes the most interesting response to a problem is to build something yourself.

And in writing because the world is too strange to be understood exclusively through dashboards, architecture diagrams, and quarterly business reviews.

For now, consider this an ongoing experiment in becoming a better systems generalist.

**Gonzo is my preferred method of investigation.** Get close to the action. Question the official account. Follow the evidence, including when it becomes inconvenient. Acknowledge the observer's own biases, appetites, and absurdities. Write as though the world is happening to you, because it is.

Hunter S. Thompson remains an enduring inspiration here: not as an excuse to imitate a dead journalist's lifestyle, but as a reminder that writing can be a form of participation rather than passive observation.

## A Generalist's Curriculum

**Technology:** Cloud computing, Kubernetes, telecom infrastructure, AI, systems architecture, software, and the occasional hardware rabbit hole.

**Business:** Products, strategy, entrepreneurship, consulting, finance, and the peculiar incentives that make organizations behave the way they do.

**The Human Condition:** History, geopolitics, philosophy, economics, political institutions, and the stories societies tell themselves.

**Culture & Creation:** Gonzo journalism, creative nonfiction, music, literature, public speaking, and the pleasures of wandering without an obvious destination.

I don't claim mastery of all these subjects. Curiosity isn't a credential, and knowing a little about everything is not the same as understanding anything deeply. But moving between disciplines often reveals connections that specialization alone can miss.

## A Personal Operating Philosophy

> "When the going gets weird, the weird turn pro."
>
> — Hunter S. Thompson

And, for a more ambitious definition of a well-lived life:

> "Specialization is for insects."
>
> — Robert A. Heinlein, *Time Enough for Love*

The point isn't to do everything. It's to remain capable of learning almost anything, to know when expertise is necessary, and to resist allowing a professional title to become the boundary of one's intellectual life.

I'm interested in competence without intellectual provincialism, ambition without unnecessary solemnity, and a life that leaves enough room for experimentation.

Coffee, books, good arguments, long walks, complicated systems, and the occasional detour into the absurd.

That seems like a reasonable place to start.
